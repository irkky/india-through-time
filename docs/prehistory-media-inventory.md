# Prehistoric India: visual evidence inventory

Reviewed 9 November 2026. The chapter retains 24 chronological entries. Eighteen entries now have local image galleries, including the two six-image Attirampakkam collections. Placeholder tags render only where evidence, attribution or reuse permission is still genuinely unresolved.

## How to explore

Scroll through **What happened**, **Why it matters** and **Evidence & limits**, or select a stage. The third stage now presents **Watch video** throughout Prehistoric India; YouTube uses a privacy-enhanced, click-to-load player and always retains a direct source link. Where a gallery exists, use **View images** / **Watch video** and the arrow controls. Open the evidence dialog to select every image, read complete captions, and follow source or licence links. Images load locally; no third-party video loads automatically.

## Sources, rights and chronology

The [media manifest](prehistory-media-manifest.json) records each local file, original download URL, source, creator credit, licence, SHA-256 digest and event placement. Captions distinguish multi-period plates from a single dated assemblage. Modern photographs and research publication dates are not prehistoric event dates. The overall journey still ends in 2025. The 2026 Brahmagiri paper investigates a prehistoric feature; it does not add a 2026 timeline entry.

- Mehrgarh figures are CC BY-NC-ND 4.0: supplied unchanged for noncommercial use. Keep complete figures and attribution; adaptations or commercial reuse require separate permission.
- Brahmagiri is CC BY-NC-SA 4.0: retain attribution and the noncommercial/share-alike conditions.
- Bhimbetka is CC BY-SA 4.0; its capture date is 2022, not the date of the painting.
- The identified Rock Shelter 8 context photograph is CC BY-SA 3.0; its capture date is 2013 and it does not date the paintings.
- The Burzahom vessel photograph is CC0. Its catalogue description associates the vessel with Burzahom and about 2700 BCE; the ASI report remains the source for pit architecture and settlement phases.
- The Salem museum display is CC BY 4.0 and the Ophelia S collection photograph is CC BY-SA 4.0. Their catalogue labels associate the tools with Attirampakkam, but neither source supplies layer-level provenance or an individual age.
- Singi Talav, Dhaba and the Bates regional map are CC BY 4.0. PLOS figures retain the Creative Commons Attribution statement from their individual articles; no unverified licence version has been invented.
- Previously supplied Lower/Middle Palaeolithic images still need their original provenance and reuse terms. They are not silently relicensed by this update.

## Candidates deliberately not substituted

The supplied Sandhav landscape and tool montage remain unused because their source, object provenance and reuse terms were not documented; the tool montage cannot safely be represented as an excavated assemblage. The supplied Burzahom roofed-house illustration is a reconstruction, not stratigraphy. The two files labelled for Chopani Mando could not be matched to that site and remain unused. A Commons Navdatoli goblet dated 1300 BCE does not match this entry's 1700–1500 BCE range. The remaining requests identify the actual site and phase needed rather than filling gaps with lookalikes.
