# Second Urbanization & Empires: media and evidence

Research and integration: **28 October 2026**. The chapter retains its **17 records** and
chronology, with **18 distinct local images**, including **11 additions**, and **55 detailed
reading sections**. Every record now has a relevant gallery. Several photographs deliberately
serve more than one theme; their captions keep the scope of that comparison explicit.

This is a documented expansion, not an exhaustive archive. A photograph does not automatically
resolve missing stratigraphy, dates, translations or measurements.

## Research sources and interpretation

- [Taxila, UNESCO](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/139/) and the
  [Bhir protected-site record](https://doam.gov.pk/public/sites/9752) distinguish the earlier
  Bhir settlement from later Sirkap. No university building is identified from the photograph.
- [Kumhrar, Government of Bihar](https://tourism.bihar.gov.in/en/destinations/patna/kumrahar-puratav-park)
  describes the pillared hall separately from the later monastery-hospital. Its differing
  functional descriptions are not collapsed into a definitive palace reconstruction.
- [The Met's Kalpasutra folio](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/74993),
  accession 55.121.38.18, and [sermon sculpture](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/38123),
  accession 20.43, are explicitly later representations. Their dates never replace the
  timeline's approximate religious-historical settings.
- [Acaranga I.4.1, Jacobi's 1884 translation](https://original.sacred-texts.com/jai/sbe22/sbe2220.htm)
  and [SN 56.11, Thanissaro's translation](https://accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/sn/sn56/sn56.011.than.html)
  provide identified reading passages. The site paraphrases briefly and links the editions;
  it does not reproduce long translations or claim contemporary transcripts.
- [The Edicts of King Asoka, S. Dhammika](https://accesstoinsight.org/lib/authors/dhammika/wheel386.html)
  remains a named translation, with interpretive differences acknowledged. Girnar XIII,
  Shahbazgarhi VII/XII, Lumbini and Dhauli are not interchangeable copies of one inscription.
- [Sanchi, UNESCO](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/524/) supports the distinction between
  the early foundation and subsequent additions. Reusing its photograph at the dynastic
  transition illustrates continued patronage at that site, not one unified successor empire.

## Video and other media

[Watch the UNESCO/NHK Sanchi film](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/524/video) from the Sanchi
reading entry. It is modern heritage footage, with **all rights reserved** by NHK. The app
links to the publisher instead of downloading, embedding or autoplaying it. External playback
and availability remain under the publisher's control.

No suitably verified, reusable ancient-period audio was found. A modern recitation would need
its performer, tradition, passage reference, recording date and permission clearly supplied.
No generic soundtrack, generated monument or invented ancient portrait is presented as evidence.

## Provenance and reuse

The [download manifest](empires-media-manifest.json) records source URLs, download URLs,
creators, licences, captions, assigned events and SHA-256 hashes for the eleven additions.
The original seven image credits remain in the [chapter guide](second-urbanization-content.md).

New Commons photographs use CC BY 2.0, CC BY 3.0, CC BY-SA 4.0 or a photographer's public-domain
release. The two Met images are public domain under the museum's Open Access programme.
The historical inscription plates are public domain. All credit and licence links remain in
the gallery. Files are unchanged originals or publisher/Commons-provided derivatives; the
Mahavira image is the museum's painted-panel detail, not a complete manuscript spread.

The Girnar plate's Commons record mixes publication metadata for Cunningham and Hultzsch.
Its caption therefore identifies the historical facsimile without asserting an unverified
edition year. Shahbazgarhi's plate explicitly identifies Hultzsch's 1925 edition.

Candidates excluded during research included an unreviewed modern Sanchi plan licence and
a Kandahar image with missing source information. These gaps remain tagged rather than
being filled by an uncertain image. A former Sarnath museum domain returned unrelated content
and was not added as a source.
