# Early Modern India: video guide

Reviewed **4 November 2026**. Thirty-two selected original-publisher videos accompany
all five periods and fifty-four entries. Videos reused in relevant periods produce
forty-two reading-section cards. All selected uploads currently permit embedding.

## Selection

Long university lectures provide the introductory explanations; museum and heritage
films offer focused visual companions. A separate video was not invented for every
entry. Cards explicitly distinguish precise subject matches from broader background.
This is a curated search, not an exhaustive index of all available videos or a claim
that every full lecture was watched and independently fact-checked.

## Period-by-period fit

1. **Ports, communities and competing states:** begin with commerce in an already connected Indian Ocean. Lectures on Mughal foundations, Sher Shah, Sikhism and Vijayanagara accompany the separate records; Deccani art and Goa heritage add visual context.
2. **Imperial consolidation and regional voices:** Akbar, religious debate, mansabs, agriculture, markets, painting, devotion and textile workmanship receive distinct guides. Humayun's tomb and Fatehpur Sikri are short UNESCO/NHK visual companions. Royal-women material is not used to explain rural women's lives.
3. **Courts, craft and cities:** Jahangir/Shah Jahan, Nur Jahan, painting, trade, the Taj Mahal, Red Fort and kalamkari. The V&A coat is embroidered, not the gallery's dyed cloth. Anna Dallapiccola's talk examines comparative painted hangings, not the exact cover pictured, and includes a Sri Lankan comparison within its South Indian textile subject.
4. **Contested power:** Aurangzeb, Shivaji, Maratha administration, Sikh traditions, Deccani memory and a full DD Assam Lachit documentary. Commemoration and interpretation are distinguished from contemporary evidence.
5. **Regional states and Company transition:** begin with interpreting eighteenth-century India, then explore regional institutions, Jaipur, its observatory, Travancore, Bengal and trade. Plassey, Buxar and Diwani remain separate dated records. Broader lectures may continue beyond 1765; those events are not moved into this chapter.

The Colachel programme is in Tamil. Other audio and caption options depend on the
publisher; no blanket English-language or caption availability is promised. Publisher
spellings and suffixes such as `SL` are preserved without inventing their expansion.

## Research and source checks

Public YouTube searches were refined by subject and institution. Original watch metadata
and oEmbed titles were checked for every selected upload. The
[manifest](early-modern-video-manifest.json) records publishers, durations, publication
timestamps, verification date, embedding evidence and event/period mappings.

Brief catalogue promotions and anniversary news snippets were replaced by substantial
lectures or documentaries where available. The Met's 36-second Deccan catalogue preview
was replaced by Navina Haidar's longer Deccan Heritage Foundation talk; a 59-second Lachit
news item was replaced by DD Assam's documentary. A foreign-trade upload with a mismatched
Europe course label was not selected. The focused Bengal lecture and Indian trade lectures
provide the Company transition. The one-minute Jaipur recognition film is retained only
as a labelled visual companion alongside the longer eighteenth-century guide.

Museum footage does not automatically match every displayed object. Promotional dates,
instrument counts, political superlatives and territorial claims were not copied into
historical facts. Existing cited timeline content and dates remain unchanged.

## Playback and maintenance

Select **Load video player**, then Play. Existing privacy-enhanced YouTube players load
only on request, with no autoplay or eager thumbnail/provider requests. Loading another
player unloads the previous one; closing the player or evidence dialog stops it. Each
card keeps a direct **Watch on YouTube** link for blocked or unavailable playback,
including without JavaScript. No automatic redirect interrupts reading.

No video was downloaded or rehosted. Embedding permissions can change. Existing heritage
reference links remain available. Video rights are separate from image licences.

- `src/data/timeline/early-modern/videos.ts`: catalogue and explicit event/period mappings.
- `early-modern/events.ts` and `early-modern-india.ts`: attach guides to existing content.
- Shared `HistoricalVideos`: player behavior; no duplicate chapter-specific player.
- `tests/early-modern-videos.test.cjs`: full coverage, metadata consistency and focused placements.

Run `npm run check`, `npm run build`, start the production server and run:

```powershell
$env:VIDEOS_CHAPTER = 'early-modern'
node scripts/browser-videos.cjs http://localhost:3131
```

The optional browser test checks all forty-two card instances at four widths, deferred
loading, direct links, a single player, dialog cleanup and no-JavaScript access. Its live
sample is CEC's lecture on Akbar's religious views; it does not test every recording in full.
