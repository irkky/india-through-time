# Indian Independence Movement — video guide

Researched and checked on **4 November 2026**. This selection adds **28 distinct videos** across
**four periods and all 29 dated records**. The reader shows 29 cards because the CLPR constitutional
overview also accompanies two different periods. Earlier milestones remain in the Colonial Era;
this chapter retains its thematic chronology and related-event links.

## Selection and verification

Searched original YouTube results for each topic, comparing university channels, public broadcasters,
museums, scholarly talks, news explainers and educational lectures. This is a curated selection,
not a claim to have found every available recording or watched every lecture in full.
Publisher watch-page metadata and descriptions were examined; each selected video returned
`status=OK`, `playableInEmbed=true`, and a matching YouTube oEmbed title. Actual availability can
vary with geography, account restrictions and subsequent publisher decisions.

Longer topic-specific material was preferred over short promotional clips. University and
public-broadcaster coverage was supplemented with clearly identified commercial teaching
lectures where a suitable institutional explanation was not found. Short introductions and
archival clips are labelled as such. No recordings are downloaded or rehosted.

## Historical scope and remaining gaps

- **Ghadar and Chittagong:** modern animated teaching, not archival footage. Overseas organisation and places now in Bangladesh remain relevant to the history of undivided India.
- **Champaran, Ahmedabad and Kheda:** one comparative lecture accompanies three distinct disputes. It does not merge their causes or dates.
- **Vaikom and Mahad:** modern reporting accompanies caste-equality history. Road access at Vaikom is not interchangeable with later temple entry. The Mahad upload uses centenary language, but the campaign remains dated 1927.
- **Kakori:** the selected DD introduction is brief; a longer independently sourced trial history remains desirable. Bhagat Singh’s biography does not supply full separate accounts of Rajguru or Sukhdev.
- **Purna Swaraj and Karachi:** CLPR provides an accessible constitutional overview. Its compressed chronology must be read alongside the dated records. Huntley’s archival compilation combines Karachi 1931, Faizpur 1936 and a Bombay ceremony; its description incorrectly dates Pakistan to 1948. It is not evidence for the resolution’s exact wording.
- **Khudai Khidmatgar:** the Manchester-hosted scholarly lecture gives regional and religious context. The 1931 timeline date is the documented Congress association, not the movement’s foundation.
- **Round Table:** British Pathé shows Gandhi’s 1931 arrival, not the negotiations themselves. A full dedicated conference lecture would complement this primary visual source.
- **Poona Pact:** the CLPR interpretation should be compared with the agreement and Ambedkar’s writings; it is not archival footage. Its [publisher page and transcript](https://www.constitutionofindia.net/video/why-did-b-r-ambedkar-accept-the-poona-pact/) are available from the card.
- **Gaidinliu:** the English Doordarshan documentary supplies biography; 1932–47 dates imprisonment, not uninterrupted campaigning.
- **Congress Radio:** the selected DD account is retrospective. The existing tagged request for an authenticated 1942 bulletin or recording remains open.
- **Rani of Jhansi Regiment:** _Elephants Do Remember_ supplies Rama Khandwala’s later-life testimony; the CEC lecture adds INA background. Neither replaces separately dated recruitment or training records.
- **INA and naval uprising:** lectures and retrospective programmes offer interpretations. Do not turn either into a single-cause explanation of independence.
- **Objectives Resolution:** the [CLPR animation and transcript](https://www.constitutionofindia.net/video/the-making-of-indias-constitution/) span the constitutional journey. Read the 13 December 1946 debate separately. The animation’s later 1950 context does not create a new dated event in this chapter.

The original five film/archive references in `independence/evidence.ts` remain available.
The IWM naval-era newsreel is contextual official publicity, not footage of the uprising itself;
its original publisher link is retained instead of forcing it into a YouTube player.
Existing image, map and research-placeholder requests remain intact.

### Alternatives not selected

The candidate Home Rule and Nehru Report uploads from Mrunal returned HTTP 401 during oEmbed
verification; publicly verifiable alternatives were selected. A Prasar Bharati Bhagat Singh
candidate also failed that check and was replaced by the Films Division upload. A CEC
`Purnaswaraj` result carried an unrelated Western Philosophy description, and the broadcaster’s
combined Chauri Chaura programme described the post-1959 Swatantra Party alongside 1920s events;
both were excluded. Very short Ghadar, Usha Mehta and Abdul Ghaffar Khan clips were replaced with
longer explanations. These exclusions do not imply that every rejected recording is unavailable
to every viewer.

## Playback and maintenance

Select **Load video player**, then Play. Privacy-enhanced YouTube embeds load on demand,
without autoplay or eager thumbnail requests. Opening another player stops the previous one;
closing the evidence dialog unloads it. Every card retains **Watch on YouTube**, including
without JavaScript. A blocked player does not automatically redirect the reader.

Vedaranyam is a Tamil-language programme; the Ghadar, Chittagong and Nehru Report introductions
are Hindi. Several selections explicitly offer English explanation. Subtitles and language
tracks depend on the publisher and are not guaranteed.

- `src/data/timeline/independence/videos.ts`: catalogue and explicit period/event mapping.
- `docs/independence-video-manifest.json`: provenance and verification metadata.
- `tests/independence-videos.test.cjs`: coverage, exact mapping and archival-context checks.

Run `npm run check`, `npm run build`, start the production server, then run:

```powershell
$env:VIDEOS_CHAPTER = 'independence'
node scripts/browser-videos.cjs http://localhost:3134
```

This optional browser test exercises all reader cards at four widths, source links, deferred
loading, one active player and dialog cleanup. Its live sample is Vidya-mitra’s Home Rule lecture;
it does not play every recording in full.
