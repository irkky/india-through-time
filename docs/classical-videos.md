# Classical & Medieval India: video guide

Reviewed **4 November 2026**. Twenty-five selected videos cover four reading sections and
all twenty-two timeline records: **24 on-demand players and one external 360-degree tour**.
The selection combines long university lectures with short site films and an exact-object
museum imaging clip. It is not an exhaustive catalogue of every available video, nor a claim
that every full lecture has been watched and independently fact-checked.

## Why these sources and placements

- **Regional kingdoms and Buddhist art:** Vidya-mitra's political overview accompanies the Indo-Greek and Kushan records. UNESCO/NHK supplies a concise Ajanta film. Early caves and later paintings remain separate phases; Gupta-era wording does not imply Gupta patronage of all caves.
- **Gupta-era India and learning:** a longer CEC introduction, a short Nalanda film, NPTEL's technical Aryabhata lecture, a South Indian Bhakti lecture and the Met's exact-object X-ray sequence. The latter shows bronze 69.222 and is only 24 seconds; its linked [museum explanation](https://www.metmuseum.org/essays/3d-xray-bronze-buddha) supplies the interpretation. The 2019 study and 2022 upload are modern research dates.
- **Regional India:** begin with a lecture on historical sources. Individual films accompany Ellora, Mahabalipuram, Pattadakal, Khajuraho, Chola temples, Rani-ki-Vav and Hoysala architecture. NPTEL explains Brahmagupta in depth. Modern tourism and heritage reporting are identified as such. The Hoysala report includes thirteenth-century Somanathpura beyond this period's start-date grouping.
- **Sultanates and regional societies:** longer lectures on Delhi, its economy, Bhakti, Sufism, historical sources and Vijayanagara accompany short Qutb and Konark films and Hampi footage. The Sultanate-sources lecture is methodological background for Ibn Battuta, not a dedicated itinerary documentary. Hampi's television film focuses substantially on the sixteenth century and is explicitly a later-context companion; the chapter still ends at 1500.

The selected lecture titles, publisher descriptions, durations and original sources were
checked. Short heritage films do not substitute for detailed scholarship. Captions and audio
languages depend on the publisher; no blanket availability is promised. Broad claims in
promotional descriptions were not copied into timeline facts, including dynasty-founder
claims, colonial rediscovery narratives and unqualified historical superlatives.

## Embedding and fallback

Every selected original YouTube watch page reported `status=OK` and `playableInEmbed=true`,
and its oEmbed response matched the title. The [manifest](classical-video-manifest.json)
records these checks, publication timestamps and event/period mappings.

Gujarat Tourism's Rani-ki-Vav upload also reported **360-degree video playback is not supported
on this browser**. Its card therefore uses external mode and opens the original video;
embedding permission alone does not prove browser compatibility. The remaining twenty-four
cards reuse the existing privacy-enhanced YouTube player. Select **Load video player**,
then Play. Players and thumbnails are not fetched on initial page load and do not autoplay.
Loading another player unloads the previous one; closing the player or evidence dialog stops it.
All cards retain direct source links usable without JavaScript. No automatic redirect interrupts reading.

The Met and Pattadakal videos were traced through their official page's player references,
not anonymous reuploads. Some direct heritage-page requests returned access errors; successful
public page reads and original YouTube publisher records were used instead. Existing UNESCO
reference links and the Nataraja audio/transcript link remain available. No recording was
downloaded or rehosted; image licences do not apply automatically to video/audio.

## Maintenance and verification

- `src/data/timeline/classical/videos.ts`: catalogue and precise record/period mappings.
- `classical/events.ts` and `classical-medieval.ts`: attach selections to existing content.
- Shared `HistoricalVideos`: player behavior; no separate chapter-specific player.
- `tests/classical-videos.test.cjs`: complete coverage, manifest consistency, focused mappings and the external 360-degree case.

Run `npm run check` and `npm run build`. Start the production server and run:

```powershell
$env:VIDEOS_CHAPTER = 'classical'
node scripts/browser-videos.cjs http://localhost:3130
```

The optional browser test checks every card at 1440, 768, 390 and 320px, blocked-provider
fallbacks, deferred loading, one loaded player, dialog cleanup and source links without
JavaScript. Its live sample is the CEC Gupta lecture; complete playback of all videos is not tested.
